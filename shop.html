Shop

Finished work, sold outright: models, music, footage, design and printed goods. Each is published wherever that kind of thing is already bought, so you can use an account you have rather than opening another one. Some of it you download, some of it arrives in the post — the sections below are sorted by which.

3D

Models built to a brief and published wherever people buy them. What you get is a file you download.

Fab

Epic’s marketplace, and where the old Unreal Engine Marketplace and the Sketchfab store were folded together. The one to use if you are building in Unreal.

CGTrader

A large independent marketplace with broad format coverage. Usually the quickest route to a specific file format.

TurboSquid

The oldest of them, and the one most companies already have a purchasing relationship with. If procurement is involved, it is the path of least resistance.

Unity Asset Store

The half of the games market Fab does not cover. Anything built for Unreal has a Unity counterpart worth publishing.

itch.io

Where independent developers actually shop, and the least corporate of them. Good for asset packs and for anything playable.

Sketchfab

Not a shop — the store side moved to Fab — but the best place to judge the work before buying it elsewhere. Every model turns and zooms in the browser at full resolution, which shows topology and texturing that preview renders hide.

Sound

Music and sound design, licensed for use in other people’s work. A track written once is sold for as long as it is listed.

AudioJungle

Envato’s audio marketplace, and the busiest single destination for royalty-free music and effects.

Pond5

Music, effects and footage in one place, which suits a studio that produces both.

Artlist

Subscription rather than per-track licensing. A different buyer: production companies who license continuously rather than per project.

Splice

Loops, one-shots and sample packs — raw material for other producers rather than finished cues.

Bandcamp

For anything that is a record rather than a licence. Sold direct, to listeners instead of to editors.

Film & photography

Footage and stills, licensed the same way. Material already shot for past projects is inventory that currently earns nothing.

Adobe Stock

Sits inside the software most buyers already have open, which is most of why it sells.

Shutterstock

The largest general library, and the one corporate buyers default to.

Storyblocks

Subscription-based, aimed at people producing volume on a fixed budget.

Artgrid

Curated and cinema-leaning. Higher bar to get in, less competition once you are.

Design

The reusable half of design work: fonts, textures, templates, mockups, icon sets, and the colour grades already built for film work.

Creative Market

The most designer-facing of them, and the best fit for fonts, textures and brand kits.

Envato

GraphicRiver and Elements together reach the widest audience for templates.

Design Cuts

Curated bundles rather than an open marketplace. Fewer listings, better margins.

AI

The tooling itself, sold as a product rather than used as one. This is the only category on the page where the studio has an advantage most sellers do not.

Civitai

Where trained models and LoRAs are actually distributed and discovered.

Workflow packs

ComfyUI graphs, prompt sets and pipelines, sold direct. What takes a client three weeks to work out is a file here.

Print

Artwork on things you can hold. What you get is an object, made and posted when you order it.

Redbubble

The wider catalogue: prints, apparel, cases, stickers and homeware, each made and shipped when someone orders it.

Threadless

Apparel first, and a more curated shopfront. The same artwork, chosen for the things people actually wear.

INPRNT

Gallery-grade art prints. Lower volume than Redbubble, considerably better margin and considerably better paper.

Displate

Metal posters, and a buyer who is decorating a room rather than buying a novelty.

Society6

Homeware and furnishings — the same artwork on objects the others do not make.

Etsy

The one place on this list where a physical 3D print can be sold as a finished object rather than as a file.

3D printing

The bridge between the two halves of the page: files meant to leave a printer as objects.

Pinshape

Files prepared to come off a printer as an object rather than to sit in a scene. You download it and print it yourself.

Shapeways

The same files, printed and posted for you, in materials a desktop machine cannot manage.

Direct

Everything above is someone else’s platform, taking a cut and owning the customer. This is the studio’s own counter — the only one where the buyer is a buyer rather than a stranger a marketplace lent out.

Gumroad

Sells any digital file with almost no setup, at a fraction of a marketplace cut. The fastest way to have a shop of your own.

Payhip

The same idea with EU VAT handled for you, which matters when you are selling from Germany.

Not on the shelf

Everything above is finished work, already made. If what you need does not exist yet, that is commissioned rather than bought — Work is what the studio takes on, and Contact is the way in.
